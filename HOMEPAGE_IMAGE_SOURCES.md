Archival source derivative: `public/homepage/roman-baths-beirut.jpg`

Subject: Roman Baths, Downtown Beirut.

Photographer: Niels Elgaard Larsen, Wikimedia Commons.

Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BeirutRomanBaths.jpg

Licence: CC BY-SA 3.0.

Homepage treatment: WebP derivative resized to 2,200 by 1,467 pixels. The desktop crop preserves the baths, the retaining walls, and the empty modern walkway. The mobile focal point moves to 56 percent horizontally so the hypocaust and surrounding city remain visible.

Reason selected: this frame explains the essay immediately. The earlier image showed only the repeated hypocaust columns and made the site look like industrial debris.

## Rejected homepage images

- The earlier Roman Baths close-up was accurate but too abstract for the lead.
- The Nahr Ibrahim valley was geographically relevant but visually generic.
- The AI-generated mourning procession collapsed different periods and religious traditions into one invented scene, so it is unsuitable as editorial evidence.
- The pigeon tower image is memorable but too seductive to lead the recent essays and too easily mistaken for documentary photography.
- Census maps remain evidence images and must use `contain`, never blind cropping.
- The Marwahin image remains out of the homepage because the visible car and fence do not explain the Rubble Zone argument.
